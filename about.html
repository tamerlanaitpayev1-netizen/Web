Exhibit: Fountain Hook

The Fountain Hook is a Dota 2 mechanic exploit involving Pudge's Meat Hook and Chen's Test of Faith teleportation. When Pudge throws a hook and is simultaneously recalled to the fountain by Chen, the hooked enemy is dragged across the entire map into the fountain. The result: instant fountain laser demise.

The technique reached legendary status during The International 2013 (Na'Vi vs TongFu, Game 3) and was eventually patched by Valve.

Exhibit: The 322 Incident

On 14 June 2013, during the StarLadder StarSeries tournament, Alexei 'Solo' Berezin — the mid-player for RoX.KIS — placed a $100 bet on the opposing team (zRage) at odds of 3.22. RoX.KIS lost the match. Solo collected $322.

The bookmakers flagged the bet. The scandal became the biggest story in esports that month. Solo received a lifetime ban (later reduced). The number 322 entered permanent Dota vocabulary. Today, it is typed whenever any player makes a suspicious play, throws a teamfight, or dies in a way that seems intentional.

"One number. One bet. Infinite accusations for every player who has ever thrown a game since."

StarLadder StarSeries — RoX.KIS vs zRage, June 2013.

Exhibit: Give DIRETIDE

In 2012, Valve launched the Diretide Halloween event for Dota 2. The community loved it. On 31 October 2013, Valve did not release Diretide.

What followed was one of the largest community campaigns in gaming history. The hashtag #GiveDiretide spread across Reddit, Twitter, and Steam reviews. Players flooded the Volvo car subreddit with demands — despite the Swedish car manufacturer having zero connection to Valve or Dota 2. Valve eventually brought Diretide back that same year (2013). The event later returned in 2020 and 2022.

Exhibit: The Cliffteezy Phenomenon

In competitive and ranked Dota, Artour 'Arteezy' Babaev became associated with a specific type of terrain incident: getting stuck on a cliff with no way down. The phenomenon has been documented across multiple matches and seasons, including the Shanghai Major 2016 era. Community members coined the term "Cliffteezy" to describe any instance of a carry player becoming immobilised by terrain.

Exhibit: Techies Demolitions — Psychological Warfare

No hero in competitive esports history has caused as much existential dread as Techies. Before their rework, a single Techies pick transformed a standard 35-minute match into a 110-minute mine-sweeping psychological horror simulator.

Opponents were forced to spend thousands of gold on Sentries and Gem of True Sight, yet still detonated on remote mine stacks placed in high-ground chokepoints. Nine other players in the lobby suffered while Techies placed bombs and laughed.
